Waypoint v1 — visual language

The house style. Waypoint is a white page with soft pastels and one blue that means “this is yours to do”. Playful but calm; a planning tool, not a chat app. The living version of this is the code: tokens in ventures/waypoint/apps/web/src/app/globals.css, components in ventures/waypoint/apps/web/src/components/ui.tsx.

See also the higher-level design approach — the principles this token/component layer serves.

The direction, in one paragraph

A white surface floats on a soft off-white canvas, separated by the faintest hairline. Corners are large and rounded — “soft toy geometry”. Each area of the app carries its own pastel so you know where you are without reading the tab. A single blue is reserved for one meaning: something that is yours to act on — a button, your own row in a ledger, a trip waiting on you. Motion is quiet: a small lift on hover and nothing more. This replaced an earlier paper-and-biro notebook look; see the decision note for why it changed.

Colour

Every colour is a token in globals.css on :root, re-exported through @theme inline as Tailwind utilities. No component ever holds a hex value — that rule survives the redesign unchanged.

The blue — “yours to do”

TokenHexRole
blue#4E68D8actions, links, your own rows
blue-soft#E7EBFAthe tint those sit on
blue-deep#2B3B85ink on the tint

Blue has exactly one meaning: this is yours to act on. A primary button, a link, your own row in the money ledger, a trip that is blocked on you. Do not spend blue on decoration — the moment blue means two things, it means nothing, and the “waiting on you” signal is lost. This is the direct descendant of the old biro-blue accent rule, tightened.

Four pastels, one per domain

Each area of the app owns a pastel, with a darker ink pair for text on it. The colour tells you which part of Waypoint you are in before you read a word.

TokenWashInkDomain
peri#DFE3FF#33409Bdates
mint#DCEFE4#1B6B4Cmoney
butter#FBEAC8#8A6412ideas and people
blush#FADDD6#A34A31route and places

Keep the pastels vivid enough to read as chosen, not washed out to grey — a bland pastel reads as unfinished. But they stay quiet next to the blue: pastels are where-you-are, blue is what-to-do, and the blue must always win the eye. Peri and blue are deliberately close — peri is a dates wash, blue is an action; never put a peri surface and a blue button so close that the wash reads as a disabled button.

People live in the butter domain. Ideas and the people who vote on them share one colour. Within a single roster you still have to tell members apart at a glance, so a member's avatar carries a per-seat tint within butter rather than eight unrelated pastels — the exact tinting is settled in the roster/overview page ticket, not prescribed here. Colour is a recognition aid and never the only way to tell two people apart; the initials and the title still do that.

Status is never colour alone

A three-state meaning — agreed / open / needs action — applied identically on every tab, exactly as before. Every status also carries a word and, where useful, a drawn glyph. “Needs action” is the blue “yours to do” state. The mint-in / blush-out pairing on the money tab is the one at real risk of reading as colour-only — it must always be labelled.

Typography

Three faces, strict roles — the cap of three survives, the faces change. All declared in :root; a fourth needs an argument, not a preference.

  • Display — Bricolage Grotesque. A characterful grotesque for headings and short display lines. Used with restraint: tighten tracking (about −2 to −4%) and line-height (~110%) on large sizes.
  • Body — Instrument Sans. A plain, legible sans for prose, controls and everything a user reads carefully.
  • Mono — DM Mono, tabular figures. Every figure, date, time and short label — all money, all balances. This is what keeps columns of numbers lining up and a ledger legible.

The legibility rule that survives from the old system, restated: money, dates and balances are always the mono face, never the display face. The display face carries personality in headings; it never carries data.

Shape and motion

Soft toy geometry

Large radii and pill-shaped controls. Where the old paper look was “cut, not moulded” (radii 3–6px), the new look is the opposite: generously rounded surfaces, and interactive controls (buttons, nav, chips) are full pills. Exact radius steps are set in tokens in the components ticket; the rule is “soft and rounded, consistently”.

One shared --ease and --dur token; every interactive surface still has a visible hover (a whole card lifts, a chip takes a border, a link takes the blue) — a cursor change alone is never the whole affordance. Nothing animates under prefers-reduced-motion.

Navigation and chrome

Pill-box navigation, everywhere. The primary nav is a row of pills; the active one is filled. The wordmark sits left, the account controls (explore, your name/avatar) are right-aligned. Same chrome on every page so the app never feels like it switches products between tabs — the continuity point the design approach insists on.

Light only

There is no dark mode, and it stays a decision rather than an omission. No dark palette in globals.css, no data-theme attribute, no theme picker on /settings, no theme column on user_profile. A dark OS preference is ignored. (The reasoning is no longer “a dark notebook is a different product” — it is simply that one well-made light theme beats two half-made ones for a pre-MVP tool. Don't reintroduce dark without a ticket.)

Iconography — unchanged

Every icon is drawn line-art, in the app's own hand. No emoji, anywhere. This rule is not affected by the redesign and is restated in full because it is load-bearing. An emoji is someone else's artwork: full-colour, rounded, different on every OS, and it cannot take the ink of the thing it sits in. Three glossy pictograms in a row are the fastest way to make the app look like a chat app.

The house treatment, shared by TravelModeIcon, ReactionGlyph and any new glyph set:

  • A 14×14 viewBox, rendered at about 13px, fill="none", strokeWidth ~1.15–1.25, round caps and joins. A glyph that needs to be bigger scales its stroke with it.
  • stroke="currentColor", always — one glyph can be ink in a row, blue in a link, or a domain ink on a wash without a second copy of the file. A hardcoded icon colour is the same mistake as a hardcoded hex.
  • Paths in a plain module, no "use client" — composes into a server or client parent. Never an icon font, never Feather / Lucide / Heroicons — those are drawn for a sans-serif product UI and read as the “generic default icon set” tell.
  • No icon carries meaning alone — it sits beside its word, or the word is in the row's accessible label; the decorative copy is aria-hidden.

Behavioural rules that survive the reskin

These are direction-independent — they were true on paper and stay true on white. They travel with the components, not the palette.

  • One visible action per row. Where a row had two or more verbs, they go behind a single triple-dot menu, revealed on demand. A ledger is mostly read; a line of buttons down its right edge competes with what the row says. Keep one action visible only where it plainly dominates (a standalone Join).
  • A thread is a disclosure, not a feed. Discussions (ideas, day events) are collapsed behind a summary carrying the count (“3 notes”), oldest-first; expanded only where you've clicked in.
  • A day event opens up. Collapsed it is one line — the sequence is what you read at a glance; open it holds its note, deep link, thread and controls. Controls live in the open panel, never in the summary row (a button inside a <summary> toggles the disclosure as it fires).
  • Lists whose order is the point are drawn as a line, not a stack. Route's stops run down one spine through numbered nodes; the node that draws the order is the node you grab; ↑/↓ are the keyboard path.
  • Nothing is written until you press save on the availability calendar — one action per editing session, not one per day.

The dates calendar

AvailabilityCalendar (client) keeps its interaction model — a month grid you paint free days onto by click or drag, with a Mine / Everyone / The dates switch onto the same grid, a counted heatmap, and the trip's own window. The interaction decisions carry over unchanged (drag paints the whole span from its anchor; pointer captured to the whole calendar so touch works; below sm: only the first month renders and arrows page the rest; the focus ring is on the mark, not the cell). What changes is only the skin: peri is the dates domain, so the wash and marks are drawn in peri and blue rather than the old highlighter green. The exact marks are set in the dates page ticket.

The Days calendar is a real calendar

Days is a working calendar, not a styled list: a week view with a real time grid (an hour gutter, events positioned by their start and end), a month view, prev/next paging, click an empty slot to add, and an event detail panel. It must still respect the domain rules: day-first, never a stop table (rule 3); an undated trip has no calendar and needs its own empty state (rule 9); no timezones, dates are YYYY-MM-DD and times are local (rule 10). Whether the month view earns its place for a one-week trip is an open question to settle in that ticket, not a foregone conclusion.

Maps and pictures

A map stays a house treatment over plain OpenStreetMap tiles — one filtered map look, not a second tile provider (keeps the “free for the MVP” decision intact), and OSM attribution stays visible wherever tiles are. The old filter knocked OSM back to ink-on-cream to sit on paper; on the white ground it is re-tuned to sit on canvas and carry a domain tint where it belongs (blush, for route and places). The exact filter stack is set when the route/map surface is reskinned. A picture sits in the same plain rounded frame — never a novelty frame (the perforated-stamp frame was rejected on sight and stays rejected).

Component inventory

Hand-rolled, not shadcn/ui — owning the dozen primitives keeps the density undiluted. The set is unchanged by the redesign; the primitives are reshaped (radii, pills, hover), not replaced.

Server components in components/ui.tsx: Page, PageHeader, Card, CardHeader, Stack, Rule, Button, ButtonLink, Badge, Stamp, EmptyState, Avatar, AvatarRow, Field, Input, Textarea, Select, ErrorText. Client components in components/client-ui.tsx: ActionForm, SubmitButton, Sheet, ConfirmSubmit, CopyLink, Segmented, Menu. Avatar colouring lives in lib/who.ts.

The screen that carries the character

/trip/[id]/overview — it holds the day track, the roster, the route and the forecast at once, so it is where the pastel-per-domain system, the pill chrome and the three-state status semantics have to work hardest together. The rest of the app is derived from it.
