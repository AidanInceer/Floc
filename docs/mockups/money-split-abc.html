Ticket 85 · #84 Money overhaul · throwaway prototype

Splitting money between people — three directions

Each column is a whole answer: what the split model is underneath, and what the group actually sees. Common to all three, and not up for negotiation: amounts are integer minor units, and whatever the model, the result is snapshotted into expense_split rows that sum to the expense exactly and are never recalculated. Everything else here is negotiable — including whether the four split types survive at all.

Direction A

Even by default, exclusions by tap

The observation: nearly every real expense is “everyone, evenly”, and the four-way split-type picker makes you answer that question every single time. So the form stops asking. Everyone’s in; you tap someone out. Anything cleverer hides behind one link.

Model: unchanged tables. split_type stays, but the form only ever writes even (over the included subset) unless you open “Split it differently”, which reveals today’s exact/percentage/shares. Participants are just which rows get written.

Direction B

One model: shares, with amounts that self-balance

The four split types collapse into one. Everyone holds shares; even is everyone on one share. Type an actual amount against a person and they’re pinned — their number is fixed and the remainder redistributes across everyone still on shares. Percentages and exact amounts stop being modes and become two ways of typing into the same row.

Model: split_type shrinks to shares plus a per-row pin. Splits still snapshot identically, so old expenses of every type keep rendering — the four types become a read-time legacy, not a live choice.

  • Open question Do pinned amounts survive an edit to the total, or re-open?

Direction C

Itemised: split the receipt, not the total

The argument isn’t usually about the total — it’s about who had the lobster. An expense becomes a small receipt of lines, each line assigned to whoever it was for, and the per-person total falls out. The tab’s headline becomes the settle-up list: the fewest transfers that clear the board.

Model: a new expense_item child table (description, amount, participants). The items are the input; the snapshot rows in expense_split are still what balances read, so the invariant and every existing expense are untouched. Even split remains the one-item case.
