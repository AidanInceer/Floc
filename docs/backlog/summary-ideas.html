Docs / Backlog

Waypoint — ideas, deduplicated and prioritised

A single review surface merging the two idea sources into one flow:

  • backlog/ideas.md — the raw brain-dump (20 high-level ideas + a free/paid feature split).
  • product-ideas.md — the problem-framed backlog with its own shortlist and a "what not to build" list.

Duplicates are collapsed. Everything is sorted into two tracks — Free (core planning, ships inside v1 shape) and Paid (AI, automation, premium tools) — and ordered top-to-bottom by the priority I'd implement them in (value against effort, staying inside the current product shape).

Nothing here is committed. Short-term bugs/polish live in tickets.md, not here.

⚑ Read the flags first. A handful of brain-dump ideas directly contradict the "what not to build" boundary in product-ideas.md. They're parked in § Contested at the bottom rather than ranked, so a real decision gets made instead of quietly assumed.

🆓 Free track — core planning

Ordered highest value-to-effort first. Items 1–8 are the ones that turn a planning tool into something a group actually uses on the trip.

# Idea The problem it solves Effort Source
1 Fronted / deposit expense flag Someone fronts £400 for the house and feels awkward chasing. Flag it "fronted" → enters the ledger immediately with a "3 of 7 squared up" state. Best value-to-effort on the page. ▁ tiny both
2 Packing & jobs list "Who's bringing the speaker / booking the transfer?" One flat list per trip, each line claimable by a member. Makes the product feel finished. ▁ tiny both
3 Fast expense capture Nobody fills a form at the table. Amount → one tap "split with everyone here" → done. Receipt photo optional, attached later. Pure UI on the existing money model. ▁ tiny product
4 One-screen "today" view The app goes quiet the moment the trip starts. A "today" screen: day-plan, tonight's address, who's arriving, one tap to log spend. Offline-readable. Strategically the retention hook. ▂ small product
5 Budget before booking Groups discover they can't afford it after booking. A per-person target ("~£600 each") + a running estimate as stops are added. Visibly an estimate. ▂ small both
6 Who's arriving when Seven people, five flights, all in the chat. An arrivals/departures board — times only, free-text, no flight API. Answers "who shares the transfer?". ▂ small both
7 Where everyone's sleeping The route says "Taormina, 3 nights" but not which house, whose name, what door code, who's in the annexe. A lodging record on a stop + a plain room-allocation list. ▂ small both
8 When the plan breaks The ferry's cancelled and seven people learn separately. Mark a day event "changed" with a one-line reason. A note, not a notification system. ▁ tiny product
9 Options, not just ideas Voting fails for "which of these three houses". A comparison card: 2–4 options side by side (price pp, distance, sleeps) → vote. Free-text rows the group defines. ▃ medium product
10 Commitment state The gap between "yeah I'm keen" and "I've booked" is where trips die. Per-member interested → in → booked, self-declared. Overview: "5 of 7 in, 2 booked". ▂ small product
11 Search across the archive "Where was that bar in Ortigia?" is buried in last year's trip. Search across every trip you've been on. Cheap; the long-term retention story. ▂ small both
12 Clone / blueprint a past trip Start a new trip from a past one (or a friend's), then customise. Pairs with the archive search above and with /explore. ▂ small both
13 Durable travel group The trip is a one-off; the group isn't. Save the roster and carry settled balances forward so last year's debt isn't forgotten. Cross-trip balances are a real data-model call — decide early. ▃ medium both
14 Quiet voices Two people decide everything; one hates the plan silently until day three. A light anonymous "does this work for everyone?" check. Easily creepy — research before design. ▃ medium product
15 Profile page + world map A proper profile: a world map of countries visited (green) and want-to-visit (yellow), shareable with friends. ▃ medium ideas
16 Profile picture Straightforward avatar upload. ▁ tiny ideas
17 Interests / vibe tags — actually used The vibe-preference and interest/category tags exist but do nothing. Wire them into pre-creating trips with friends. ▂ small ideas
18 Dietary requirements Per-member dietary flags, surfaced where the group books food. ▁ tiny ideas
19 Parent / carer role A member who can add children to the trip, distinct from member/admin. ▂ small ideas
20 Reminders (non-nagging) A light reminder system to aid planning without feeling like chasing. Fits "nudges, not nagging". ▂ small ideas
21 Basic weather Near-term forecast on the trip. (AI weather-aware planning is the paid version — item P6.) ▂ small ideas

💳 Paid track — AI, automation & premium

Bigger bets and the tier that justifies a subscription. Ordered by the "magic per unit effort" that would make someone pay.

# Idea What it does Notes Source
P1 Import booking confirmations from email Parse flight/hotel/car confirmations from the inbox and auto-fill the trip. The strongest "this feels like magic" automation; feeds arrivals, lodging & budget for free. Privacy-heavy — scoped, explicit consent. ideas
P2 Visa & passport checks From nationality + destination, tell each member whether they need a visa, link the source, and flag passport expiry. A free self-declared checklist summary can live in the free tier; the automatic lookup is paid. Data must be current or it's a liability. ideas
P3 AI packing recommendations Given destination, dates & travel style, generate a packing list — light vs heavy — and de-duplicate across the group (one speaker, not seven). Builds on the free packing list. ideas
P4 AI trip summaries A readable summary of the plan / a trip recap. Low-risk, high-delight entry AI feature. ideas
P5 Flight tracking + live delay alerts Track booked flights, notify on delays/cancellations, optionally nudge an itinerary reshuffle. Needs push — out of v1 scope; a paid-tier reason to add it later. both
P6 AI weather-aware planning Flag when a planned clear-sky activity hits a cloudy/wet day and suggest reordering. both
P7 AI conflict resolution "Find a compromise everyone will like" across the group's stated preferences. Must augment the group's own decision, not replace its legitimacy. ideas
P8 AI schedule rebuilding Auto-rebuild the day plan when something breaks (delay, closure). Pairs with P5. ideas
P9 AI suggestions — destinations / restaurants / activities Recommendations seeded by the group's tags and budget. See flag C1 on generated-itinerary legitimacy. ideas
P10 AI budget optimisation Suggest where to trim to hit the per-person target from free item 5. ideas
P11 Advanced voting Ranked-choice, preference weighting, scenario comparison, alternative-itinerary generation, budget simulations. Premium layer over the free comparison card (item 9). ideas
P12 Premium maps Offline maps, custom pins, collaborative route editing, walking routes. ideas
P13 Automatic syncing Calendar sync of the itinerary; auto-categorise expenses. ideas
P14 Premium travel tools Currency tracking, airport terminal & lounge info, check-in reminders, local transport suggestions, busy-period/holiday flags for the destination. Grab-bag; cherry-pick, don't build wholesale. ideas
P15 Driver's licence checks Like the visa check — whether your licence is valid to drive at the destination. ideas

⚑ Contested — decide before building

These come straight from the brain-dump but collide with the boundary already set in product-ideas.md (§ "What to not build") or CLAUDE.md v1 scope. Ranked nowhere on purpose — pick a side first.

# Idea (from ideas.md) Why it's flagged
C1 AI travel agent that plans trips on your behalf / AI itinerary generation product-ideas.md argues generated plans have no social legitimacy — the hard problem is what seven people will agree to, not what's possible in Sicily. Keep AI as an assistant to the group's decision (P6–P10), not an itinerary vending machine.
C2 Photo journal / Polarsteps-style social sharing (auto-syncing phone photos to trip days) Directly contradicts "no public trip sharing / social feed" — a different product wearing this one's clothes, and it flips the privacy model from "nine people" to "the internet". A private post-trip photo book is a possible middle path; the public feed is not.
C3 Shared live location Explicit "don't": the phone already does it, no planning value, privacy liability.
C4 Flight & hotel booking in-app Out of v1 scope — regulated and commission-driven. Search deep links only, paying nothing.
