What Waypoint is missing

Grouped by strategic priority for a group-travel planner at pre-MVP stage.

Priority 1 — Core group planning gaps (high impact, in-scope)

Feature Who has it Why it matters
Activity voting Wanderlust The #1 group friction: agreeing on what to do. Async voting unlocks this without chat chaos.
Day / route optimiser Wanderlust, Wanderlog Groups waste significant time manually reordering stops. A one-tap optimise is a clear "wow" moment.
Simplified debt / settle up Splitwise Waypoint splits expenses but doesn't collapse the web of IOUs into minimum transfers. Groups need this.
Multi-currency & currency conversion Splitwise International trips involve multiple currencies; single-currency money tracking is a hard limit.
Split modes (%, shares, unequal) Splitwise Equal split isn't always fair — couples, mixed groups, and shared accommodation all need flexibility.
Read-only sharing link Wanderlust, TripIt, Wanderlog, Polarsteps Let people view a trip without needing an account. Critical for sharing with less-tech-savvy members.

Priority 2 — Strong differentiators worth adding (medium-term)

Feature Who has it Why it matters
Email import of booking confirmations Wanderlust, TripIt, Wanderlog Forward a hotel confirmation and it appears in the trip. Removes manual data entry for booked trips.
Offline trip access Wanderlust (Pro), TripIt, Wanderlog (Pro), Polarsteps Travellers go offline. A cached copy of the itinerary is table-stakes for a travel app.
AI itinerary builder Wanderlust, Wanderlog, Polarsteps "Tell me what to do for 5 days in Lisbon" is the first thing a user wants. Currently requires manual building.
Packing checklists Wanderlog Small feature, high utility, low build cost. Keeps users in the app pre-trip.
Scheduled visit times / travel times Wanderlust, Wanderlog Knowing how long to get between stops is essential for day planning.
Flight status & alerts TripIt (Pro), Wanderlog (Pro) High-value for frequent travellers; builds trust as a reliable travel companion.

Priority 3 — Longer-term / post-MVP

Feature Who has it Why it matters
Travel Passport / visited-country stats Wanderlust, Polarsteps Gamification + memory layer. Drives retention and social sharing long after a trip ends.
Curated city guides / discovery Wanderlust, Wanderlog Answers "where should we go?" before a trip even starts. Acquisition channel as much as a feature.
Community trips / clone-a-trip Wanderlust, Wanderlog UGC flywheel — shared trips drive new-user discovery.
Bucketlist Wanderlust Persistent pre-trip wishlist. High retention value; surfaces as reminder next time a user is near a saved place.
Browser extension Wanderlust, Wanderlog Captures intent at the moment of inspiration (article, Instagram). Hard to build but powerful.
Live travel / Trip Mode Wanderlust Real-time "what's next" on the ground. Significant build; more relevant once core planning is solid.
GPS tracking Polarsteps Polarsteps' core moat. Adds memory/journalling angle; complementary to planning rather than core to it.
Printed travel book Polarsteps Monetisation and keepsake. Long-tail; only relevant once users have completed trips.
Receipt scanning Splitwise (Pro) Quality-of-life for expense entry on the go.
Calendar sync TripIt Useful for individuals; less critical for a group-first product.

Strategic positioning summary

Waypoint's current coverage is solid on collaborative planning and trip management (itinerary, members, money basics, overview) but thin on the features that make a group choose it over existing habits:

  • Groups already use Splitwise for money → Waypoint needs richer splitting to displace it
  • Groups already use WhatsApp/Messenger for decisions → Waypoint needs voting to replace that thread
  • Groups use Google Sheets or Wanderlog for itinerary → Waypoint needs route optimisation and offline access to win on utility

The defensible position is: one app that handles the whole group trip — planning, decisions, money, and sharing — without needing Splitwise + WhatsApp + a separate map tool. Every feature gap above is a reason a group member keeps one of those other tabs open.
