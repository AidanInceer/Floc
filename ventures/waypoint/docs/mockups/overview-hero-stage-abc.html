Where the trip is up to — three ways to say it

Today the hero opens with a typed “Up to”, then the derived stage sentence in the page’s largest type — which for a finished trip is “Ended — still editable if anything’s unfinished”. The label and its caveat are fighting for the same line. Each direction below keeps everything the sentence has to convey (what stage the trip is at; that an ended trip isn’t locked) and moves the pieces around differently. Each is drawn mid-plan and ended.

Direction A

Drop the kicker, split the caveat off

“Up to” goes: the sentence was never ambiguous without it, and a two-word label above a headline is the cheapest thing on the panel to delete. The stage sentence gets shorter — “Ended” is the stage — and the caveat drops to a quiet line beneath in body type, where a reassurance belongs. Smallest change of the three; nothing moves except what the words are.

Direction B

The trip is the headline; the stage is a badge

The largest type on the page goes to the thing that never changes shape — the trip’s name and its dates — and the derived stage demotes to a badge beside it, in the vocabulary the rest of the app already uses for state. The ended caveat becomes one plain sub-line. This is the direction that stops the hero re-flowing every time the stage sentence changes length, at the cost of the stage no longer shouting.

Direction C

Say what to do next; the stage is the small print

The hero answers the question a member actually arrives with — what needs doing — and the stage becomes a typed line under it, next to the ended marker when there is one. The stage is still derived from exactly the same rows; it just stops being the headline. Boldest of the three, and the only one that needs a rule for what “next” is when several things are open at once.
