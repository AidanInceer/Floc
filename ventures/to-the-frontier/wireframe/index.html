Unit 3 · Lesson 5

Longer chains don't buy accuracy for free

4 min read · then 3 questions · builds on “sampling temperature”

A reasoning model writes intermediate tokens before it answers. It's tempting to read that trace as the model thinking out loud — and to assume that more of it means a better answer. The measured picture is messier.

Past a task-dependent point, extra steps mostly add places for an early mistake to compound. The trace stays fluent, so the failure is invisible from the outside.

The trace is evidence about the answer, not a transcript of the reasoning that produced it.
[ figure — accuracy vs. trace length, plateau then decline ]

The practical move is to sample several short chains and take the majority answer, rather than paying for one very long one.

You double the reasoning budget on a maths benchmark and accuracy drops. What's the most likely cause?
A The model ran out of context window
B Early errors propagated through the longer chain
C Temperature was set too low
D The benchmark is contaminated
