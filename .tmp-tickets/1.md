## What to build

A person who opens a trip's share link can take part straight away: give a name, then vote on the destination and mark the dates they can make. The account is asked for after, not before. When they sign up, the votes they cast as a guest become theirs.

Today the invite page shows the itinerary read-only and every action needs an account. Each trip puts several non-users on this page; this is the largest conversion step Floc has. Rests on Less friction; must keep Traveller trust.

## Decide in grilling (needs an ADR)

- Which actions a guest may take: destination vote, date availability, both, or more.
- How a guest is identified without an account (name only, a browser-held token) and what the group sees next to a guest's vote.
- How guest votes join an account on sign-up, and what happens to them if the person never signs up.
- How this sits with rule 5 (enumeration-proof access) and ADR-018 (the link is the credential): a guest writes to a trip they are not a member of.
- Abuse: a forwarded link must not let a stranger flood the vote. Rate limit, cap, or admin removal.
- What a guest still cannot see: roster, money, notes, member email, files stay closed.
- The phone: does the app need the same, or is this web only (declare in parity.json).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ADR records the decision; the landing FAQ answer "Does everyone need an account?" matches it
- [ ] A signed-out person on the invite page can cast a vote with a name and no account
- [ ] The group sees the guest's vote, marked as a guest
- [ ] After sign-up from that invite, the guest's votes belong to the new member, with no duplicate
- [ ] A guest reads nothing the invite page does not already show
- [ ] Guest writes are rate limited and capped
- [ ] Any new browser-stored value is listed on /privacy in the same commit

Flag: touches auth and trip access.
