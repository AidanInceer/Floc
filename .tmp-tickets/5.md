## What to build

A member connects their own calendar and Floc marks the days they are free or busy across the trip's candidate dates, so the group sees when everyone can go without each person filling it in by hand. First version: Google Calendar only. The member reviews what was filled in and can change any day.

The partner of #327 (itinerary out to a calendar); this one brings availability in.

## Prototype first

Answer with a throwaway prototype: does a busy day in a calendar mean the person cannot travel? A two-hour meeting, an all-day event and a holiday already booked are different. Find the rule that gives an answer people trust.

## Decide in grilling

- One-off import or a kept connection that refreshes.
- What is read: free/busy only, never event titles, places or guests. Nothing from the calendar is stored beyond free or busy per day.
- What counts as busy (all-day events only, events over N hours, working hours).
- Imported days are a suggestion the member confirms, or written straight to their availability.
- Dates have no timezone (rule 10): how a calendar's timed events map to a trip day.
- Google OAuth: a calendar read scope is a sensitive scope and needs Google's app verification before real users can grant it — time this against go-live.
- Without Google credentials the control hides (rule 11).
- Later providers: Apple, Outlook, an .ics upload.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A member can connect Google Calendar and see their free and busy days filled in on the trip's dates
- [ ] Only that member's own availability changes; they can edit every imported day
- [ ] Floc stores no event detail, and the token is revocable from settings
- [ ] The group sees the days everyone is free, as today
- [ ] With no Google key the feature is hidden, not broken
- [ ] The privacy page lists the data read and any new stored value, in the same commit
- [ ] Present on web and phone, or the gap declared in parity.json with a why

Flag: touches auth, OAuth tokens and personal data.
